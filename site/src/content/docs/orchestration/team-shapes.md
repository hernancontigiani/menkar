What changes from one project to another is how much the orchestrator itself knows, and so how
much the [experts and consultants](/menkar/orchestration/roles/) beside it carry.

## Start with the orchestrator alone

For a small project, talk only to the orchestrator. It designs the work, hands each task to a
worker and reviews what comes back.

## Add experts for the parts that recur

When one part of the system comes back again and again, such as releases, give a session a role
and its own skill, and hand it to the orchestrator. The orchestrator gives it the work of that part
and hears when it finishes, so you need not watch both. Workers still come and go for everything
else.

## Add consultants for the areas the orchestrator does not know

When the work spans an area the orchestrator does not know, often another repository, give a
session a role and leave it outside the team: a consultant. For work in that area the orchestrator
asks the consultant for the key points, starts a worker, and tells the worker to ask the consultant
its questions. The consultant can review the result, and stays free to think with you.

With no consultant for an area, the orchestrator asks you. When the same kind of question keeps
coming, make a consultant for it.

## Run workers on a smaller model

When a consultant answers the workers' questions and reviews their work, the workers can run on a
smaller model. Write which one in the project's instructions; the orchestrator never picks one by
itself.

## Keep experts and consultants current

Have each one's skill hold ways of working and point to the project's documents, not copy their
facts; a worker that changes a feature then updates its document in the same change. When what
changed alters how an expert or a consultant works, ask it to load `menkar:distill` and update its
skill.
